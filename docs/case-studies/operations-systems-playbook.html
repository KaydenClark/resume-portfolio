What this demonstrates

This is a safe, synthetic example of the work behind the case studies on this site. It shows the artifacts a business process analyst creates so a workflow is not dependent on memory, a single spreadsheet author, or a chain of inbox messages.

The scenario is fictional: a monthly service-billing workflow needs a controlled path from submitted hours to approved invoice data. Names, dates, amounts, and records below are invented.

Workflow Requirements brief Data dictionary UAT checklist Production SOP

1. Workflow and control points

The goal is not to remove human judgment. It is to make the required judgment, owner, and evidence visible before a billing record becomes final.

  1. Intake. The operator loads an unchanged source export and records the reporting period, source date, and file owner.
  2. Validate. Required fields, duplicate rows, rate mappings, and period totals are checked before any calculations are used downstream.
  3. Reconcile. Approved adjustments are matched to source hours. An unresolved variance remains an exception, not a hidden formula result.
  4. Approve. The reviewer receives a draft detail, exception list, and explicit approval state. A final cannot be issued while a required approval is missing.
  5. Publish and hand off. Final data, an audit record, and the next-run checklist are stored together so another operator can repeat the cycle.

2. Requirements brief

Business problem
Monthly billing depends on multiple inputs, approvals, and adjustments that are easy to lose in manual handoffs.
Primary users
Operations analyst, billing reviewer, account owner, and accounting recipient.
Success condition
Every billed row has a source record, valid rate mapping, approval state, and an explainable exception path.
Non-goal
The system does not replace financial approval or permit a user to silently override an unresolved variance.
Acceptance criteria
Invalid rows fail visibly; period totals reconcile; approval is recorded; final output is reproducible by another trained operator.
Dependencies
Source export availability, approved rate table, adjustment log, reviewer availability, and accounting delivery deadline.

5. Production SOP

  1. Confirm the period, source owner, and expected delivery date before loading data.
  2. Save the unchanged source export and record its receipt in the run log.
  3. Run required-field, duplicate, mapping, and period-total checks. Resolve or log every exception.
  4. Prepare the draft detail and reviewer summary; do not create a final while approval state is incomplete.
  5. After approval, issue the final output, archive the exception log, and document any change to the mapping or process.
  6. At the next run, start from the checklist and prior change log rather than reconstructing the process from memory.

Why this matters

A useful operations system makes the work easier to inspect, easier to hand off, and harder to get quietly wrong. The spreadsheet, workflow tool, or platform can change; the operating discipline should remain.
