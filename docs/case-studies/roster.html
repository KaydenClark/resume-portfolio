Roster System: 279 People On — and Off — in Six Weeks

  • 279 people / 6 weeks
  • Scales from 279 heads to 3
  • Co-author
  • Portfolio-wide standard

The situation

A seasonal wagering client needed a massive short-term ramp: 279 people hired, onboarded, and then off-boarded within six weeks. Every one of those moves had to be coordinated across IT (provisioning and deprovisioning), recruiting, and my internal operations team — and every headcount number had to survive an audit, because roster data feeds staffing plans, cost allocation, and client billing downstream.

Constraints

  • Three departments, one truth. IT, recruiting, and operations each act on roster changes; a missed term date means an account left active or a paycheck error.
  • Loss can't be fatal. Hire and term data had to be recoverable even if the roster itself was damaged or overwritten.
  • Scale both directions. The same process had to work for a 279-person surge and for a 3-person specialty health campaign — without feeling like bureaucracy on the small end.

What we built

A master roster per campaign with a triple-validation loop:

  1. Real-time updates. The roster is the live source of truth; changes land as they happen.
  2. Monthly validation. A scheduled full check of the roster against reality, on every campaign, every month.
  3. Email confirmation trail. Critical changes — hires and terms — also go out by email, creating an out-of-band backup that can restore lost data, and an audit trail of who knew what, when.

The design principle: everyone who needs to know gets informed by the process itself, not by someone remembering to tell them. The small campaigns are where informal process quietly rots — so the 3-person campaign runs the same loop as the 279-person ramp.

Why it matters

Roster data is the upstream dependency nobody owns until it breaks something expensive. The triple loop treats data loss and human forgetfulness as expected failure modes, not surprises: real-time for speed, monthly for drift, email for disaster recovery. It worked across a 93x scale difference because the process was designed around how it fails, not how it's supposed to go.

Impact

  • 279 / 6 weekspeople ramped onto — and fully off — a client account, coordinated across IT, recruiting, and operations
  • Every accountthe same loop is the standard across the portfolio I manage, from 279 heads down to 3
  • Reconstructablepoint-in-time headcount recoverable from the email trail — protecting billing periods and audits
  • Validated indownstream systems (cost allocation, invoicing) consume confirmed counts, not self-reported ones

Artifacts

  • Synthetic roster data dictionary — required fields, ownership, and validation states
  • Change-control SOP — real-time update, validation, exception, and confirmation steps
